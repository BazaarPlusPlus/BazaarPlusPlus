# Android Mobile UI

How every BPP surface on the Android host fits a phone: the touch menu and its BPP button, Collection, History (with its Ghost section and replay entry), Ten-Win Builds, the rows BPP adds to the game's settings dialog, and the upgrade and enchant previews in the game's own tooltip. The host itself is [android-host.md](android-host.md); the reason for one shared frame is [ADR-0018](../adr/0018-android-touch-surfaces-share-one-frame.md).

## The shared frame

`Core/Ui/TouchMetrics` turns the screen (size, `Screen.dpi`, `Screen.safeArea`) into the overlay canvas's units. The canvas scales with the screen's height (`AndroidUiKit.CreateOverlayCanvas`), so one dp is a different number of canvas units on every screen; layouts always ask `TouchMetrics.Dp`, `Sp` and `Target`, never a constant in canvas units. A device that reports no density reads as `TouchMetrics.FallbackDpi`.

`Core/Ui/TouchChrome` is the frame every surface lays out in: the sheet (the safe area less a margin), its body (less a padding), the gap, the header row, the type scale, and the close button. Each panel's own layout (`CollectionTouchLayout`, `HistoryTouchLayout`, `LiveBuildTouchLayout`, `TouchLauncherLayout`) starts from it and places only its own content. `TouchChrome.Inspect` is the tap-to-inspect sheet all three card panels share (`TouchInspectLayout`). All of it is Unity-free and tested in `tests/SettingsDockCore.Tests/TouchChromeTests.cs` on the screens below.

## Conventions

- **Targets.** Every button, tab, chip, row, and search field is at least `TouchMetrics.MinTargetDp` (48 dp) tall, and a close button is `TouchChrome.CloseWidthDp` wide. A layout that runs out of room shrinks rows down to that floor and never further (`TouchLauncherLayout`). A card socket a tap picks is a target too: Ten-Win Builds keeps every socket at least 48 dp wide, and where its boards beside the overview would leave one narrower, or the overview's column too short for the metric cells (`LiveBuildTouchLayout.MinMetricCellDp`), as on a dense screen, it stacks (`LiveBuildTouchLayout.Stacked`): the final build's title band and close stay as a header and the boards, then the overview, scroll under it. The game's own settings dialog keeps its native rows and grows their hit areas instead (`TouchMetrics.MissingTargetPixels`, android-host.md).
- **Text.** No text on a BPP surface is under `TouchMetrics.MinBodySp` (12 sp); `TouchMetrics.Sp` refuses less. Text BPP adds inside the game's own tooltip follows the tooltip instead (Previews, below). The scale is `TouchChrome`'s: a title, a card name, a button or chip, body text, in falling order. An `AndroidUiKit` button's label sits `TouchChrome.LabelInsetDp` in from either side, the same dp on every screen; History's buttons keep their own palette's padding. A chip's label shrinks to fit (TMP autosize) between the button and body sizes, then ellipsizes; it never wraps. The Collection inspection measures its labels at body size first and drops chip columns until the widest shows whole (`CollectionTouchLayout.InspectChipColumnsFor`), so an enchantment name is never cut; a filter chip whose label would not fit its span widens across more columns (`TouchChrome.ChipSpan`). Text that would not fit grows its band (History's board messages) or takes a shorter touch label (Ten-Win Builds' `LiveBuildPanelText.CandidateTouchTag`). A sideways-scrolled strip holds only chips: a note in one runs past the screen's edge, so it wraps in a column with room instead (History's Ghost cloud note, in the rail column Ghost leaves empty, `HistoryTouchLayout.CloudNote`).
- **Safe area.** Everything lays out inside `TouchMetrics.SafeArea`, so a cutout or rounded corner never covers a control. The BPP button sits on the safe area's left edge, centered at `TouchLauncherLayout.LauncherCenterShare` of the screen height: the game scales its HUD by height, so its left-edge controls keep their share at every ratio, and that band lies between the in-run day and hour dial and the main menu's MENU button. `TouchLauncherLayoutTests` holds the button clear of those controls, measured on the device, at every screen below.
- **Sheets.** A panel is a full, opaque sheet: title top-left, close top-right (`TouchChrome.Close`), content below. A dialog closes from the same corner of its own body (`TouchChrome.CloseAt`). Every close button is `AndroidUiKit.CloseButton` or, on History's own palette, its `AndroidUiKit.CloseGlyph`.
- **Back key.** Every surface closes on the back key, top-most first; the chain and how it reaches BPP before the game's escape menu are [android-host.md](android-host.md)'s.
- **Off switch.** A feature's switch must be reachable while its surface is open. The BPP button hides while History is open, so History's More dialog carries the panel's own off switch; it writes the setting its settings row writes (`android.settings.changed ... source=history_more`) and closes the panel with its cards returned.
- **No hover, right-click, or hotkeys.** A tap replaces hover: tapping a card opens the inspect sheet with the card enlarged and its text beside it; a tap outside the info column, its close button, or the back key dismisses it. Every action the desktop puts behind a hotkey or a context menu is a visible button.
- **Scrolling.** Ten-Win Builds fixes the recommendation and one lower board on ordinary landscape screens; a button beside the lower board cycles current board, stash and shop without clearing candidates. Both boards have native frames with inset card and tag areas. Recommendation paging joins the board switch in the right operation column; hero identity moves into the recommendation title. A compact library row sits below the metrics and above recommendation paging: muted status on the left opens scrollable corpus details (including freshness and errors), and a native framed Update button on the right refreshes the library. Paging and the board switch expand into the remaining height, with the final switch aligned to the lower board frame rather than the scroll viewport. The board column needs no separate switch band. Empty recommendation guidance occupies the upper board itself. Its dense arrangement scrolls the selected board and overview under the header. Lists and grids scroll by drag with inertia (`AndroidUiKit.VerticalScroll`, the Collection grid's included; History's own palette has `AndroidHistoryUi.Scroll`); `Ui/AndroidScrollTracker` logs each finished gesture (`android.history.scrolled`, `android.collection.scrolled`) with its distance, peak speed and coast frames.
- **Columns from width.** A card grid chooses its column count from its width, never a fixed desktop count: `CollectionTouchLayout.GridColumns` keeps a small item's column and a skill's square above a readable minimum, up to the desktop's count, and never fewer columns than a large item spans. `CollectionGridLayout.Build` takes that count; the desktop overload keeps `CollectionGridConstants`.
- **Relayout.** A surface lays itself out again when `AndroidTouchScreen.Key` (size, density, safe area) changes, and renders the same state into the new tree; an open panel stays open (`android.collection.relaid_out`, `android.ui.touch_layout`). Collection closes an open inspection first (`android.collection.inspection_closed reason=relayout`), returning its card. History reopens an open note dialog on its unsaved draft (`android.history.relayout note_draft_kept=true`). Ten-Win Builds, which rents its cards again on a relayout, waits for the key to hold still and for no card to be setting up (Core's `TouchRelayoutDebounce`, capped so a stuck setup cannot pin the old layout); `android.live_build.relayout` logs the frames it held. Each panel's view goes through Core's `TouchViewSlot` (`src/BazaarPlusPlus.Core/Ui/TouchViewSlot.cs`): the old view is forgotten and dropped before the new one builds, and the key is recorded only once the build returned. So when a build fails, on a relayout, on opening, or for Collection's first tree, no panel keeps a destroyed view: the build destroys its partial tree, the panel logs `android.collection.relayout_failed`, `android.live_build.layout_failed` or `android.history.layout_failed`, reads closed (a failed relayout requests the host's close, so the touch launcher comes back), and builds again on the next open. The recovery stays the panel's own: Collection's close ends its view state, Ten-Win Builds closes its state, History's overlays go with the view.
- **History header.** Android History keeps section tabs, one supporter credit that opens the sponsor page, More and Close. The run archive sizes its rows to show three complete runs in the initial viewport while keeping the touch-target floor; additional runs remain drag-scrollable. It offers no hero, outcome or day filters; the archive uses the default unfiltered coordinator state. The supporter credit shares Collection's catalog and samples once per opening, with a late catalog arrival filling the initial sponsor label.
- **Native look.** `GameInterop/AndroidNativeArt` borrows popup button sprites and tooltip frame/TagFrame art; `Ui/AndroidNativeSkin` applies them to Collection, History and Ten-Win Builds without copying native callbacks. Panels keep opaque backing beneath transparent frame art, decorative images take no touches, and missing sprites fall back to the host palette. Reopening a panel with missing or destroyed art rebuilds its view. History keeps its warm backing and status palette, and its dimmed actions remain tappable where the model supplies an explanation. `Ui/AndroidPortraitView` shares Collection's framed hero crop with History archive rows; rebinding a reused row invalidates its previous portrait request. `Ui/AndroidInspectionText` scrolls long History and Ten-Win Builds descriptions at body size instead of truncating them.
- **Previews in the game's tooltip.** Text BPP draws at a reduced size inside a native tooltip (the enchant preview) is raised toward 12 sp but never past the tooltip's own text: where the game's tooltip text is under 12 sp, that size is the floor. The 12 sp floor is for BPP's own surfaces, because text larger than the native lines around it breaks the native look, and the tooltip's size and layout are the game's. So the enchant preview's lines keep the desktop's reduced size only while that still reads at 12 sp: `TouchMetrics.ReadablePercent` raises them from the passive label's rendered size (`android.enchant_preview.rendered` logs `effect_sp`), and stops at the game's own passive text, which on the emulator's 2400x1080 renders under 12 sp. The upgrade preview renders inside the game's own tooltip text at its size and needs no sizing of its own. While the upgrade preview holds a card, the enchant preview stays off it.

## Screens

The tests cover a 20:9 phone (2400x1080 at 420 dpi, with and without a cutout; Ten-Win Builds also at 480, 560 and 640 dpi, where it stacks), a 16:10 tablet (2560x1600, with and without the emulator's 91 px top inset), a 16:9 phone (1920x1080), and a device that reports no density. The device evidence covers the first two. On the emulator `adb shell wm size 2560x1600` switches the screen without a reboot, and the surfaces re-lay out live; always `adb shell wm size reset` afterwards.

## Gotchas

- The overlay canvas scales with height, so a 16:10 tablet has fewer canvas units across than a 20:9 phone of the same height: a layout that fits the phone by canvas units can overflow the tablet. Lay out in dp from `TouchMetrics`, and test both ratios.
- A rented card carries its own `Canvas`, so a `RectMask2D` does not clip it: a scrolled card list draws first and opaque bands above and below it hide the cards past its edges, and the inspect sheet is opaque under its info column.
- `RectTransform.rect` of a stretched child is valid as soon as its parent has a size, so a flow that measures wrapped text (`AndroidChipFlow.Note`) needs its parent placed first.
